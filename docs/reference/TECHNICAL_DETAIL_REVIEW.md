# Source-first technical-detail review

Reviewed 2026-10-23 against the maintained documentation at commit `e243431`. This review responds to LIVE-08/09: the discussion contained technical vocabulary and detail that was difficult to find, or too compressed, in the development documents. It checks the synthesis itself, not merely source availability.

## Finding

The concern was substantiated. The earlier documents preserved the main architectural responsibilities and many important failure cases, but some entries collapsed several technical distinctions into one broad point. In other places the mechanism was explained accurately under general English wording while its original API/field name disappeared. A point linked to a large section could therefore pass the documentation checker without preserving every relevant detail in that point's source.

The repair expands the owning guides and reference records. [TECHNICAL_TERMS](../TECHNICAL_TERMS.md) supplies navigation by exact names. Copying the transcript, adding names without explanation, or inflating the point count would not address the problem. No new runtime feature or final schema is selected by this work.

## Sources and method

The current chat was read directly through its paginated message interface. Actual user/assistant message bodies were available, rather than only summaries. The 205 discussion messages overlapping the preserved snapshot matched its local bodies exactly. EVT-1167/1168 are administrative export-branch messages absent from this chat's returned history. Both remain bundled. The direct EVT-0258 body is also empty.

Later human instructions about reconstruction, excluding old ledgers, self-containment and detailed explanations were reviewed directly.

Three reviewers rotated away from the module ranges they had most recently authored. They read the original substantive discussion and supplied attachments, including the controlling final consolidation, and derived candidate technical details before assessing the maintained prose. Their source ranges were EVT-0002–0414 with ATT-01–06, EVT-0415–0826 with ATT-07–20, and EVT-0827–1168 with ATT-21–30. The root reviewed direct earlier architectural/framework explanations, late corrections and the final consolidation, and integrated the shared owners/navigation.

A lexical scan of direct replies and pasted attachments helped find exact spellings absent from the baseline. This was a discovery aid only: it also found example IDs, old source paths, arbitrary variable names, already-explained aliases and rejected proposals. Absence of a spelling was not automatically a missing mechanism. Presence of a spelling was not sufficient coverage. The original point inventory was likewise an aid rather than the exhaustive checklist.

For a material detail, the review asks whether maintained prose explains:

- What the term or field means, its owner and its scope or identity.
- How the relevant input becomes an output, state change or receipt.
- The ordering, preconditions and failure behavior that distinguish the proposal from an unsafe alternative.
- The concrete example or causal reason behind a correction.
- Whether it is a retained requirement, recommended mechanism, illustrative schema, historical library observation, rejected claim or unresolved choice.

## Important details that were already adequately explained

The review did not treat every missing spelling as a lost mechanism. The baseline already explained the following and they remain in place:

- Agent relevance decisions versus app capacity handling, retained originals versus current input, and native-pixel and full/raw/range access.
- The 140k → 100k → 80k rolling-compaction counterexample, applicability versus normal-send capacity, and self-sufficient handoff versus an unsafe changing delta anchor.
- Whole accepted-batch recovery: an edit receipt does not finish a pending preview call.
- Conditional prepared-version commit and the prohibition on rewinding head when replaying an earlier operation receipt.
- Same-request frozen input, durable spending and context-maintenance calls sharing the send boundary.
- Sparse page-map updates requiring a complete merged validation, and the page-12/page-13 nested override example.
- Explicit-version export, actual member/byte checks and distinct directory/ZIP publication effects.

These adequate explanations are still useful evidence of coverage, while the added details make their implementation meaning and historical rationale easier to recover.

## What this review establishes

The new explanations and terminology navigation address demonstrated synthesis deficiencies. Their presence, link validity, source hashes and independent reading are different checks. A structural checker cannot certify that every semantic nuance was preserved, and a glossary cannot certify source fidelity or runtime behavior.

The effective architecture remains the small StateGraph, shared native Nexus/dispatch services, document/evidence core, SQLite metadata and immutable content. The three executable contracts in [OPEN_QUESTIONS](../OPEN_QUESTIONS.md) remain intentionally open. Exact library versions, fields, node/table counts and optional algorithms were not invented to make the documents appear complete. No old application status or closed-job authority was imported.

The current development record should explain substantive discussed mechanisms directly. Original bundled messages/attachments remain optional verification material. A developer does not need access to the direct chat, original export or ignored audit scratch. See [PRESERVATION_AUDIT](PRESERVATION_AUDIT.md) for the separate integrity and repository-only verification scope.
